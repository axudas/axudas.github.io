Smart Ring Modulator

Oct 2026 – Present · ESP32 · LibrePCB · FreeCAD

A guitar ring modulator pedal built around an ESP32 DevKit and powered from USB-C. A classic transformer-and-diode ring multiplies the guitar by a carrier, and the ESP32 generates that carrier, adds a digital effects mode, and runs an OLED and rotary encoder.

What I built

  • Designed the schematic and laid out and routed a 100 × 75 mm through-hole PCB in LibrePCB.
  • Analog multiplier: two center-tapped 600:600 transformers around a four-diode ring, fed by a 1 MΩ MCP6002 input buffer.
  • ESP32 square-wave carrier from DAC1 with a pot for frequency, plus a digital path (ADC in, DAC out through an RC low-pass).
  • Modeled a 3D-printed enclosure in FreeCAD.

Automatic Cat Feeder

Dec 2025 – Aug 2026 · ELEE 4230 Sensors and Transducers · ESP32

An ESP32 feeder that weighs every portion and watches my cat's water fountain. A load cell closes the loop on a servo dispenser, and water-level and turbidity sensors tell me when to refill or change the water.

What I built

  • A load-cell signal chain: the Wheatstone bridge's 3.4–4.6 mV output goes through a TL081 differential amplifier (gain of 33) on a ±4.5 V split rail, then a 1.6 kHz RC low-pass, the ESP32 ADC and a Kalman filter.
  • Closed-loop dispensing to ±5 g on a daily schedule or on demand. If the servo turns and the mass doesn't change, it raises a stuck-or-empty-hopper alert.
  • Calibrated the water-level sensor from 835 logged readings with a 4th-order fit (R² = 0.84) and a 95% Scheffé band. Tested the turbidity sensor by adding kitty litter to the water.
  • A web app hosted on the ESP32 for live status, alerts and manual feeding, reachable securely over a VPN. Readings are logged to an SD card and uploaded to ThingSpeak.
  • A 3D-printed housing and load-cell bowl mount. I switched from force-sensitive resistors to the load cell when the FSRs couldn't reach the accuracy the portions needed.

Bach: Procedural Music Generator

DawgHack 2025 · Team of three

Bach composes short, multi-instrument MIDI pieces from a few choices (tempo, mood, instruments, and how "zany" it should be) without any machine learning. Everything comes from music theory and probability.

How it works

  • Each of seven moods maps to one of the seven Greek modes, from Ionian to Locrian, with its own rhythmic feel.
  • A first-order Markov chain picks chord progressions, with separate major and minor transition tables that follow common harmonic movement.
  • Chord, bass, and lead tracks are generated separately and merged into one MIDI file.
  • Random 7ths, 9ths, octave shifts, and an adjustable chance of altered chords keep pieces from sounding mechanical.

My role: Front end and integration

  • Built the Streamlit web interface and its parameter inputs.
  • Connected those inputs to the composition engine.
  • Added in-browser playback and one-click .mid download.
